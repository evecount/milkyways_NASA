# Second farm type — Chicken farms and spare heat
Separate document, 14 Nov 2026. Not v1. MilkyWays v1 is dairy only.

## The question
What do chicken farms do with all their spare heat?

## Short answer
Mostly they throw it away, then pay to heat the next batch.

## The heat problem, in two directions
- Day-old chicks need 32-34 C, dropping to 18-22 C by market weight.
- Farmers burn propane / gas to heat the house to about 35 C at the start.
- Then the birds grow and become biological heat generators. They cannot sweat, they pant, and they produce enormous metabolic heat.
- That excess heat is usually just vented out through tunnel ventilation, to keep the birds alive. It is waste.

## What smart farms do with it
Three main ways, all proven:

### 1. Ventilation heat exchangers
Dutch company Inno+ Air WTW pushes ventilated air through a heat exchanger, and reuses that heat for floor heating, stalls, liquid feed, or the farmer's house. Reported energy bill cut: 65%.

### 2. Composting litter heat
Chicken litter composting gets to 65-70 C.
- Triea Systems, Cottage Hill Farm, West Virginia: captures compost heat and puts it back into the broiler houses, saving about 70% of propane heating costs.
- French farm, Doux broilers: 13 km of water pipes in the compost barn, underfloor heating in the next broiler house, at least 50% less gas, 26% saving on the farm's total gas bill.

### 3. Use it for the next batch
Store the birds' own heat, and the litter's heat, to warm the chicks that come after them.

## Why this matters for MilkyWays
Same pattern as dairy, different animal:
- Farm makes heat, throws most of it away, then pays for heat again.
- Farms that win are the ones that close the loop.

Chicken is a possible second farm type after dairy, if MilkyWays works. It would need:
- A different user: broiler farmer, not dairy operator.
- A different heat use: brooding chicks, not pasteurising milk.
- A different NASA angle: heat stress is the risk for chickens, not fodder rotation. NASA temperature / humidity data would warn about heat stress, not rank fodder.
